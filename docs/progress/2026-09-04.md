## Corrections to yesterday's entry

Four, and the first is the one that matters most because it weakens a claim
yesterday made about its own instruments.

**1. The clean truncation divide had luck in it.** Yesterday: "Nineteen samples
returned nothing, all of them on Qwen builds, the incumbent none in forty-five."
True as recorded. But at the raised budget the incumbent produced a `vm_trace`
sample of **7,491 tokens** — above yesterday's 6,144 ceiling, and a sample that
would have been recorded as `truncated_no_answer` had it been drawn yesterday.
Its three `hard-full` samples came in at 3,467, 3,529 and 5,069. **The incumbent
was not comfortably inside the ceiling; it was against it, and the three draws
were short ones.** The confound was on both sides of the comparison, not only
the Qwen side. One sample of four at the raised budget, so this is an
observation and not yet a rate.

**2. `ini_parse` did not reproduce a third time.** §7.4 of
[model-evaluation.md](../model-evaluation.md) says it "reproduces exactly where
the comparison lives — incumbent 100.0% in both runs, `27b-q8_0` 54.2% in
both", and calls it the first score in one task set here comparable with a score
in another.

| `ini_parse` | `qwen38` | `hard-full` | `hard-full-2` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `gemma4:31b-it-q8_0` | 100% | 100% | **79.2%** |
| `qwen3.8:27b-q8_0` | 54% | 54% | **33.3%** |

Two candidate environmental causes were checked and both are ruled out.
Generation rate is identical across all three phases to within a decimal — 13.8
/ 13.7 / 13.8 tok/s for the incumbent, 15.5 / 15.5 / 15.6 for `27b-q8_0` — and
the Ollama LaunchDaemon plist has not been modified since 2026-08-18, before any
of the three phases, so `OLLAMA_KV_CACHE_TYPE=q8_0` was in force for all of
them.

The cause is in the samples. The same **five of eight checks** fail as a block
in every failing sample of every model and every phase: `basic sections and
keys`, `comments whole-line only`, `keys lowercased, values not`, `last value
wins`, `repeated section merges, empty section kept`. It is one recurring
defect, not a gradient. The incumbent's record of producing it is 0 of 3, then
0 of 3, then **1 of 3**.

So `ini_parse` is not a broken task — it is the widest discriminator in the set
today, at 79.2 / 37.5 / 54.2 / 33.3, four candidates and four distinct figures.
What is broken is using its absolute value as a cross-phase anchor. **Three
samples cannot establish "reproduces exactly"**, and yesterday's claim that a
score here is comparable with a score there needs to be read as "the task is
carried unchanged", which is true, rather than "the number recurs", which is
not.

**3. `count_inversions` is not a control on the harness.** §7.4 carries it
precisely because it was "1.00 for every model in every round", which "says the
scorer beneath it did not move" when the budget changed. It moved: 0.80 in three
of four samples across today's two phases, at both 4,096 and 6,144, so not a
budget effect.

The scorer is not at fault and the finding is the opposite of a harness defect.
The failing check is `small cases`, with `RecursionError`, and the cause is in
the model's code:

```python
def merge_and_count(left, right):
    if left == right:          # >= is required
        return 0
    mid = (left + right) // 2
    ...
return merge_and_count(0, n - 1)
```

On an empty list `n` is 0, so the outer call is `merge_and_count(0, -1)`,
`left != right`, `mid` is `(0 + -1) // 2 = -1`, and the first recursive call is
`merge_and_count(0, -1)` again — identical arguments, unbounded recursion.
Reproduced independently outside the harness. **The scorer caught a real
edge-case defect**, which is what it is for.

What this costs is the control, not the task. A task whose pass depends on one
comparison operator is a coin flip with a model-dependent bias, and two prior
phases of three samples each drawing the good side is not evidence of a ceiling.
Nothing in this harness currently establishes "the scorer did not move" — see
below for why that is a property of the harness rather than of these two tasks.

**4. Yesterday's entry contradicts itself about `precedence_chain`.** Its
truncation tally reads "`vm_trace` nine, `ledger_replay` nine,
`precedence_chain` one". A hundred and twenty lines later it says
`precedence_chain` was "0.00 for all four candidates with every answer
*completed*, no budget involved". Both cannot hold. The tally is right — one
`27b-mlx` sample truncated, eleven of twelve completed — and
[model-evaluation.md](../model-evaluation.md) §7.6 copied the wrong half.
Corrected in place in both files today.

## The harness has never pinned a sampling temperature, and that is what all three of those corrections are

Three findings above are the same finding. `ini_parse` moving, `count_inversions`
moving, and `ledger_replay` scoring 1.00 in one pilot sample and 0.00 in the
next are all one draw differing from another.

`harness_parts/client.py` sends `num_ctx` and `num_predict` and nothing else, so
every sample is drawn at whatever the model's own Modelfile specifies —
`temperature 1`, `top_k 64`, `top_p 0.95` for `gemma4:31b-it-q8_0`. §5 already
requires several samples per task and order rotated per round, and its stated
reason is that "one roll of a sampler is not a capability". That rule was written
against *ordering* effects and it does not reach this: three rolls of a sampler
at temperature 1 are not a capability either, and the set has been reporting
three-sample means as though they were.

**Deliberately not changed today.** Pinning the temperature would make every
figure in `full`, `qwen38`, `hard-full` and `hard-pilot-2` incomparable with
everything measured afterwards, and doing that in the same phase that changed
five tasks and a budget would confound two things at once. What it needs is its
own phase, run against an unchanged set, so that the change in the temperature
is the only difference. Recorded here as the next harness question rather than
as a fix.

The immediate consequence is a reading rule: **a §4.4 verdict from three samples
is provisional.** Today's eight-signal-task ranking is the best measurement this
repository has produced and it is still three rolls per cell.

## The revision itself, and the id rule it settled

Five task ids are new in `hard-full-2`, which is why it is a separate phase from
`hard-full` rather than more rows in it.

- **Group N, all four replaced** (`determined_seats`, `undetermined_seats`,
  `undetermined_key`, `determined_key`), after scoring 1.00 for every candidate
  in every round of `hard-full`. The diagnosis they were built against: the old
  undetermined halves asked for a quantity over a subset *inside* a row, and a
  subset that visibly has no column gets refused without any reasoning about
  determinacy. The eighteen-task set's `insufficient_data` was one inference
  deep instead — the divisor was in the table and the numerator looked
  attributable — so a replacement has to make the wrong arithmetic *inviting*
  rather than absent. Pair 1 does; pair 2 does not, and the difference is worth
  keeping for the next attempt.
- **`precedence_chain` → `precedence_relief`**, five links down to three:
  4.3 → 9.2 → 12.5, stopping where the relief is found not to reach a change
  touching two services. The scenario changes in exactly one respect, so chain
  length is the only variable. §7.6 predicted the 40–70% band; it landed at 75%
  across candidates while separating the incumbent from all three challengers,
  so the prediction's number was wrong and its direction was right.

**The rule the set now follows without exception: a task whose question changed
gets a new id.** `vm_trace` keeps its id and is not an exception — one integer
moved in the program it traces, and it asks the question it asked before.
`precedence_relief` was renamed because its scenario facts, its governing clause
and its answer all changed; a table putting `precedence_chain` 0.00 next to a
later figure under that id would read two different questions as one series.
That is the error `evaluation_task_definitions` was added yesterday to make
visible, and an id is cheaper than a lookup at preventing it.

## Three harness defects found by using it

**A results row could not be placed in time.** `results.jsonl` carried `phase`
and `round` and no timestamp, so a sample could not be correlated with anything
else the deployment recorded. The question that could not be answered was
whether a gateway request had ever arrived while a phase held the runtime:
`usage_records` has timestamps and this had none. Every row now carries `at`, the
UTC instant the sample started, which with the existing `wall_s` also recovers
when it ended.

**A trap guarantees a restore begins and not that it finishes.** The wrapper
script runs `run.py restore` from `trap ... EXIT INT TERM`, which is already the
fix for a 2026-09-03 failure where a restore chained with `;` was defeated by a
`pkill -f` that matched the wrapper shell. Today it failed the other way: the
phase was killed, the trap fired, the restore *started* — the log shows it
loading `gemma4` — and then the restore was killed by the same signal, because
it was a child in the same process group. `/api/ps` returned an empty list:
every model gone, `chat`, `code`, `assist` and the embedder all down, until it
was re-run by hand.

The restore is now spawned with `start_new_session=True` so it leaves the
process group entirely, and the wrapper only watches it. macOS ships no `setsid`
binary, which is why that is a Python file rather than one word of shell.
**Verified under the failure it was built for**: the wrapper's process group was
sent `SIGTERM` and then `SIGKILL`, the wrapper died, the restore survived in its
own group and ran to completion, and the snapshot — which `restore()` deletes
only on success — was gone afterwards.

**`DEPLOYED` was missing a tag.** `/api/ps` reports `nomic-embed-text:latest`
and the fallback literal said `nomic-embed-text`, so `restore()`'s eviction
comparison never matched the embedder against itself: every fallback restore
evicted it and loaded it straight back. Harmless in effect and wrong in a way
that will matter the moment that path is trusted. The snapshot path was never
affected — it records the name the runtime gave it.

## Context ceilings, measured, and a plan for compaction

A separate piece of work, prompted by a question about what actually bounds
context on this machine and a request for automatic compaction with the switch
on the API key, defaulting on. Written up as
[plans/automatic-context-compaction.md](../plans/automatic-context-compaction.md);
what belongs here is what was measured.

**64 GiB is not the constraint on context.** `gemma4:31b-it-q8_0` was read from
`/api/ps` at two context lengths in the same session: 36,023,377,591 bytes at
`num_ctx` 262144 and 34,099,523,747 at 16384. **1.79 GiB for 245,760 tokens**,
about 7.8 KB per token, because `OLLAMA_KV_CACHE_TYPE=q8_0` quantises the cache
and `gemma4.attention.sliding_window = 1024` keeps most of its 60 blocks
window-bounded. All three deployed models resident come to 42.1 GiB of 64.

That reframes 2026-08-07. `_set_num_ctx` records Ollama *predicting* 55.8 GiB
for the same class of model at 262144 and evicting everything on the strength of
it. **The eviction was driven by a prediction, and the prediction is the thing
that was wrong** — which does not make sending `num_ctx` any less correct, but
does mean the incident is not evidence that context is expensive here. Three
figures for this model still disagree: `models.memory_gb` 41, the prediction
55.8, `/api/ps` 33.55 GiB.

**The `num_ctx / 2` rule holds, and was tested rather than assumed.** A
hypothesis that the halving was `n_ctx / n_parallel` and therefore absent at the
`-np 1` this deployment runs would have meant `max_context_length = 122880` was
refusing callers at half of what the runtime could read. A 99,271-character
prompt to `qwen2.5:7b` at `num_ctx` 32768 returned `prompt_eval_count = 16386`.
The rule stands and the ceiling is correctly placed below 131072.

**The second probe is the one to keep.** The same prompt, with
`SECRET-WORD: pomegranate-47` as its first line and a closing question asking
what that word was, returned `prompt_eval_count = 16386`, `done_reason: "stop"`,
and the answer `"the"`. The model never saw the first line, answered anyway, and
answered wrongly with nothing to indicate a loss. This is `diagnostics.py`'s
"fluent, and only wrong" reproduced on demand in one request — and it corrects
that docstring in the direction of alarm: it says `done_reason` comes back
`length`, "which is also what a generation that filled its budget reports, so
nothing downstream can tell the two apart". The measured value is **`stop`**.
There is nothing to tell apart. A silently truncated request is
indistinguishable from a healthy one.

**`--context-shift` cannot be turned off from configuration.** The runtime is
invoked with `--context-shift --keep 4`, which is already the crudest possible
automatic compaction, dropping by position with four tokens kept — not a system
prompt. `llama-server` supports `--no-context-shift` and reads
`LLAMA_ARG_CONTEXT_SHIFT`, but the `ollama` binary contains only
`--context-shift` and passes it unconditionally, a command-line argument
overrides the environment variable, and `ollama serve --help` offers no switch.
Disabling it means patching Ollama or changing runtime.

And it may be the same mechanism as the halving: llama.cpp's shift keeps
`n_keep` and discards `n_left / 2`, which is the ratio measured to within the
two tokens of `--keep 4`. **Unverified, and the largest single capacity lead
available** — if true, disabling context shift would remove the silent
truncation *and* return the other half of the context.

**What actually blocks two heavy users is `-np 1`.** One runtime slot;
`max_concurrent_inference = 4` is queue depth by its own docstring's admission.
The longest request on record is 1,311,827 ms and `queue_wait_seconds` is 120,
so a second caller is refused with `503 overloaded` after two minutes while the
first still has fifteen to run. That is one configuration value, and it is not
compaction.
